NICHE SYNTHESIS, water-storm-mitigation

Score from Module 2A: 28 / 35

End customer in one sentence: A homeowner in crisis (burst pipe, storm flooding, mold discovery) at 2 AM or any hour, panicking about their damaged home, calling multiple companies looking for the one that will answer and get on-site fast, and worried about whether their insurance will cover the work.

The student's client in one sentence: A small-to-mid-size restoration company (1-4 trucks, $500k-$2M revenue), often owner-operator, competing against Servpro/Belfor franchise machines on one side and unqualified handymen on the other, who needs a website that captures emergency inbound calls and converts them at 2 AM.

Top 5 insights:
1. Speed to on-site is the #1 trust signal. 27.8% of reviews mention speed themes. "Got to my house fast" and "On-site within 60 minutes" are the money phrases. Everything else (certifications, insurance, quality) is secondary to whether the customer can talk to a human in the next 5 minutes.
2. This is a phone-first, mobile-first business. Customers are physically in the flooded space, holding their phone, dialing. Forms are useful only for photo-uploads. The website must treat the phone as the primary CTA and mobile as the primary layout.
3. IICRC certification is table-stakes. It's the industry-standard credential. Any mitigation company website without prominent IICRC display looks unlicensed. This is the parallel to roofing's GAF Master Elite.
4. Natural bundle with storm-restoration roofing. A hail event damages the roof AND causes water intrusion. Homeowners often need both services. Alan can offer a unified "storm restoration ecosystem" agency that serves both roofers and mitigation companies as sister niches (potentially the same customer relationships on the roofer side, too).
5. Alan's warm network is actually in this niche. Per Section 4 of discovery, his ~3 warm-DM owners include mitigation contacts. This gives him a shorter path to the first mitigation client compared to cleaning.

Top 3 risks if we pick this niche:
- Restoration is dominated by two big franchise systems (Servpro, Belfor) that own the top of PPC and SEO in every market. Alan's clients are the local independents fighting those giants. Marketing has to lean into "local vs franchise" positioning.
- Emergency traffic is 2 AM / weekend / after-hours. Client must have 24/7 phone coverage. If they don't, no marketing will save them from the customer moving on to the next Google result.
- Insurance-friendly billing sounds simple but has legal complexity (some states have "assignment of benefits" laws). Alan needs to be careful about promising insurance-billing services on client sites — the client's own arrangement with carriers must be verified.

Top 3 reasons to pick this niche:
- Second-highest score in Module 2A (28/35). Highest recession-proof score (5/5) — water damage happens in every economy.
- Natural bundle with storm-restoration roofing. If Alan picks either, positioning the other as an adjacency lets him potentially serve both from one agency brand.
- Highest per-job margins in the home services universe. A single sewage backup or mold job can pay for a month of marketing services. Client retention is easier because clients see fast ROI.

Money math headline: If we get you 70 emergency leads a month at $4,000 ad + retainer spend, and your inspection-to-close is 50%, you'd close 12 emergency jobs a month at $9,500 average = $114,000 in new revenue. The system pays for itself in the first week of month two.
