# Storm-Restoration Roofing — Trust Signals

**Rank from review evidence:** 63.4% of 5,040 reviews mention trust/professionalism themes. Trust is the #2 theme after quality. In an industry with a scammy reputation (hail-chasers, door-knocking harassment, fly-by-night operators), trust signals are the #1 CRO lever.

## Top 5 trust signals to lead with (priority-ordered)

### 1. Manufacturer certifications (top spot)
- **GAF Master Elite** (top 3% of roofers nationally)
- **Owens Corning Platinum Preferred** (top 1%)
- **CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster**
- **IKO ROOFPRO Premium**

Why #1: these certifications enable the roofer to offer **manufacturer-backed warranties** (25-50 year), which is what the customer actually pays for. Anyone can claim to install a roof; only certified contractors can extend the shingle warranty. This is the single strongest differentiator vs storm chasers.

### 2. Local licensing and insurance (numbers visible)
- State contractor license number, prominently displayed
- General liability insurance ($1M+ coverage, screenshot of certificate)
- Workers' comp insurance
- Bond number if state requires
- Years in business (the "founded 2011" line)

Why #2: Fly-by-night storm chasers cannot produce these. Displaying real numbers (license #XX-XXXXXX) is much stronger than a "Licensed & Insured" text badge.

### 3. BBB A+ and Google review count
- BBB accredited business logo with A+ rating
- Google reviews aggregate: "4.9 ★ · 847 reviews" (with hyperlink to Google Business Profile)
- Volume matters more than average — 847 reviews at 4.7 beats 30 reviews at 5.0

### 4. Photo and video testimonials (real names, real neighborhoods)
- Video testimonials from actual customers (2-3 mins)
- Photo of homeowner + finished roof + first name and city
- Bonus: testimonials that mention the insurance battle by name — "State Farm denied me and Alan Roofing got the full replacement approved"

### 5. Insurance-carrier familiarity (subtle proof, big impact)
- Logos of major insurance carriers with "we work with" language: Allstate, State Farm, Farmers, USAA
- Named public adjuster relationships if the roofer has them
- "Preferred contractor" language where legally allowed

## Niche-specific certifications to look for and display

Beyond the manufacturer certs above:
- **HAAG-certified inspector** (for storm damage inspection credibility in insurance disputes)
- **CIT (Certified Infrared Thermographer)** if the roofer uses thermal imaging
- **Drone pilot Part 107 FAA** if the roofer uses drone inspections
- **State roofing contractor license** with number
- **NRCA (National Roofing Contractors Association)** membership
- **Local Chamber of Commerce** membership (for hyperlocal trust)

## Press / "as seen in" expectations

Storm-restoration is a local business — national press is nice-to-have but not decisive. What matters more:
- **Local news mentions** — after major storm events, local newspapers profile responsive roofers. Screenshot and embed.
- **Realtor referrals** — many roofers get realtor partnerships. Displaying realtor endorsements is niche-effective.
- **HomeAdvisor / Angi "Top Rated"** or similar third-party awards
- **BBB Torch Award** for ethics if the business has it

## Photo / video testimonial expectations

- Homeowner speaking to camera in front of their finished roof, 60-90 seconds, unscripted
- Split screen: before storm damage / after new roof
- Aerial drone footage of finished install for premium feel
- Text testimonials must include: first name, last initial, city, and preferably the specific concern that was addressed (e.g., "State Farm denied my claim")

## Anti-patterns to avoid (killed trust in the review data)

- Stock photos of "happy homeowners" — customers spot these immediately
- "Family owned since 1985" without a founder photo or story — feels manufactured
- Vague "insurance experts" claims without naming specific carriers
- Testimonials that use last-name-only signatures (looks like AI-generated content)
- Any pop-up on first load — negative signal for this trust-cautious audience
