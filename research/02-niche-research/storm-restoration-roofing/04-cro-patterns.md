# Storm-Restoration Roofing — CRO Patterns

## Typical section order (winning storm-restoration roofer sites)

1. **Hero** — headline + subhead + primary CTA (phone-first) + hero image (drone shot of finished roof or crew on roof)
2. **Storm-damage trust bar** — "GAF Master Elite" / "Owens Corning Platinum" / "BBB A+" / "{state} licensed & insured" — 4-6 badges
3. **Insurance-advocate section** — 2-3 line explanation of how they handle the claim + a "how it works" 3-step visual
4. **Reviews strip** — 4-6 5-star Google review snippets with reviewer names
5. **Services** — Roof replacement / Roof repair / Storm damage inspection / Insurance claim assistance (limited to 4-6; more dilutes)
6. **Photo gallery** — before/after storm damage → new roof (drone photography is standard)
7. **About/why-us** — founder photo + short story + team photo. Local roots emphasized
8. **FAQ** — insurance-focused: "Will you handle my insurance?", "What if my claim is denied?", "How long does the process take?"
9. **Service area** — cities served, map visualization
10. **Final CTA** — form + phone + text option

## Hero composition

- **Imagery:** Finished-roof drone shot (aspirational, calm) or crew-on-roof action shot (active, competent). Never a crumbling roof — that's the fear, don't lead with it.
- **Primary CTA:** phone number (large, click-to-call). Storm-restoration is high-intent, phone still wins.
- **Secondary CTA:** "Get my free inspection" form
- **Social proof inline:** "★★★★★ 4.9 average from {N} Google reviews" right below the headline

## Trust stack ordering (priority-ordered)

1. Manufacturer certifications: **GAF Master Elite**, **Owens Corning Platinum Preferred**, **CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster**
2. Local licensing: state license number, insured, bonded — displayed with numbers, not just badges
3. BBB rating: A+
4. Google reviews aggregate with number: "4.9 ★ from 847 reviews"
5. HomeAdvisor / Angi / Nextdoor badges
6. Photo/video testimonials — not just text
7. Insurance-carrier relationships (Allstate approved, State Farm preferred, etc.)

## Form patterns

- **Fewer fields wins in this niche.** 3-4 fields max on the hero form: name, phone, ZIP, "brief description of damage."
- **Phone is the primary conversion event** — the review data shows customers value being able to talk to someone same-day. Click-to-call in header is table stakes on mobile.
- **Multi-step forms perform better** for insurance-driven leads. Step 1: "Do you have insurance?" (Yes/No filter) — Step 2: contact info. Filters unqualified.

## Sticky elements

- **Mobile bottom bar:** phone + text + book (3 icons). Recover 5-10% of would-be bounces on mobile.
- **Header:** phone number always visible, even on scroll.
- **Exit intent (desktop):** "Get an emergency inspection within 4 hours" popup with just phone field.

## Divergence from the universal converting wireframe

The universal home-services wireframe puts services above trust badges. **Storm-restoration reverses this.** Trust badges (especially manufacturer certifications) belong right below the hero because customers fear scams — the whole storm-restoration industry has a reputation for hail-chasers and fly-by-night operators. Certifications neutralize that fear before the offer even lands.

Also: the storm-restoration industry is a **phone-first business**, not a form-first business. The customer wants to know they can talk to a human today. The universal wireframe treats phone as secondary; here it's primary.
