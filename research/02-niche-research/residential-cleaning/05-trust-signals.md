# Residential Cleaning — Trust Signals

**Rank from review evidence:** 53.7% of 5,396 reviews mention trust themes. Cleaning trust is different from roofing/mitigation trust — customers are letting a stranger into their home, so the trust bar is about SAFETY, not just quality.

## Top 5 trust signals

### 1. Bonded and insured
- Both are needed. Bonded covers theft; insured covers accidents.
- Display with dollar amounts if possible ("$1M liability, $50k bond").
- This is the #1 trust signal for cleaning — customers ask directly.

### 2. Background-checked cleaners
- Explicit language: "Every cleaner passes a national background check before joining our team."
- Some agencies show the specific service used (Checkr, Sterling, GoodHire).

### 3. Same-cleaner-every-visit commitment
- Customers value knowing WHO will be in their home. Franchises rotate cleaners; local independents can promise consistency.
- Named team-member photos on the site amplify this signal.

### 4. Photos of real cleaners (not stock)
- Team photos of actual cleaners with first names. Anti-anonymous, anti-franchise feel.
- Some agencies show cleaner tenure ("with us since 2019") — powerful retention proof.

### 5. Years in business + local ownership
- "Family-owned in {city} since {year}" beats "national franchise."
- This came up repeatedly in positive reviews — customers explicitly volunteer that they prefer local over franchise.

## Niche-specific certifications and badges

- **IICRC Master Cleaning Technician** — rarely used for residential but signals professional-grade
- **ISSA membership** — International Sanitary Supply Association
- **Better Business Bureau A+**
- **HomeAdvisor Screened & Approved**
- **Angi Certified**
- **Nextdoor Neighborhood Favorite**
- **State business license number**
- **Cleaning For A Reason** (charity partnership — cleans homes of cancer patients for free; big trust play with a compassionate customer segment)

## Press / "as seen in" expectations

- Local news mentions
- Community charity involvement (Cleaning For A Reason, disaster response, etc.)
- Chamber of Commerce membership
- Neighborhood Facebook group recommendations (indirect, but influential)

## Photo / video testimonial expectations

- Customer video walkthrough of finished clean, ~60 seconds
- Photo of homeowner + cleaner together (with permission) — humanizes the relationship
- Text testimonials with specific cleaner's name mentioned ("Logan is our tech and has been great")
- Before/after photos of specific rooms (kitchens, bathrooms)

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Stock photos of white women in aprons with feather dusters — 20 years out of date
- Anonymous team ("our professionals") — no names, no photos
- "No contract" as the primary trust signal — that's price/friction language, not trust language
- "Family owned" with no family photo or story — feels manufactured
- Generic "we love clean homes" language

## Key takeaway
Cleaning is a SAFETY-first trust decision. The customer is letting a stranger into their home when they're at work. Bonded + insured + background-checked + same-cleaner is the winning stack. Everything else is quality — which is table-stakes.
