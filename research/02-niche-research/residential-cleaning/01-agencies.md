# Residential Cleaning — Existing Niche-Serving Agencies

## Known agencies in the cleaning-industry marketing space (US)

### Cleaning Business Fuel (cleaningbusinessfuel.com)
- **Approach:** Cleaning-specific coaching + service. Owned by an ex-cleaning-company operator.
- **Pricing:** $1k-$3k/mo blend of coaching and marketing services.
- **Sells:** "I ran a $2M cleaning business. Let me help you grow yours." Founder-brand play.
- **What they miss:** Real website + SEO delivery. Heavier on coaching than done-for-you agency work.

### Grow My Cleaning Company (Mike Campion)
- **Approach:** Coaching, podcast, community, courses. Sells to cleaning company owners nationally.
- **Pricing:** Course + community + coaching packages, $500-$5k range.
- **Sells:** Growth strategies, culture, hiring cleaners, retention plays.
- **What they miss:** Direct done-for-you marketing services. Alan can complement here as the DFY execution arm.

### The Cleaning Growth Company / Cleaning Boss / Clean Bosses
- Various coaching + community offers. Some tack on services (websites, ads).
- Pricing varies widely.
- Weaker on tight execution, stronger on community.

### Local generalist agencies serving cleaning as one vertical
- Home services agencies that "also do cleaning" — cheap, generic, weak niche fluency
- Cheap and easy to compete against with real cleaning-industry language

## Top 3 patterns winners share
1. **Recurring-plan-first framing** — websites push recurring biweekly/monthly plans (higher LTV) over one-time cleans. Copy explains the value of the recurring package.
2. **Cleaner-quality trust signals** — "background-checked cleaners, bonded and insured, English-speaking" (customers care disproportionately about safety).
3. **Booking-online friction removal** — instant online quote with square footage input + booking calendar. Customers hate calling to book cleaning.

## Top 3 patterns losers share
1. **Ambiguous pricing** — "Contact for a quote" instead of transparent per-square-foot or per-bedroom pricing. Cleaning customers price-shop aggressively.
2. **Stock photos of white maids in aprons** — feels generic, lacks trust. Real cleaner photos win.
3. **No recurring plan structure** — treating every job as a one-time booking. Kills LTV.
