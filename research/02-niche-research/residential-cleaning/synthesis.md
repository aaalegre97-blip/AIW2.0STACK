NICHE SYNTHESIS, residential-cleaning

Score from Module 2A: 27 / 35

End customer in one sentence: A dual-income homeowner (often 30s-50s) who wants their Saturdays back, cares more about safety and consistency (bonded, insured, background-checked, same cleaner each visit) than about polish, prefers online booking to phone calls, and will happily commit to a recurring plan if the pricing is transparent and cancellation is friction-free.

The student's client in one sentence: A small-to-mid-size residential cleaning company owner (2-10 cleaners, $250k-$1M revenue), often female-led, competing against Merry Maids/Molly Maid franchises and low-price gig-app competitors, who needs a website that generates online-booking-ready recurring customers, not just one-time cleans.

Top 5 insights:
1. Cleaning is a subscription-checkout business, not a lead-gen business. The winning website has instant online quote widgets and embedded booking calendars. Every friction point on the way to the first booking is a killer. Roofing/mitigation is phone-first; cleaning is form-first.
2. LTV math is the whole game. Per-job ticket is low ($150-$250) but a biweekly recurring customer is worth $7,800-$10,400 over 18-24 months. Positioning must emphasize the recurring plan, not the one-time clean.
3. Safety trust signals dominate. Customers are letting strangers into their homes when they're at work. Bonded + insured + background-checked + same-cleaner-every-visit is the winning stack. Quality is table-stakes.
4. Alan has unique operator authority. He owns a residential cleaning company. His copy can say "I run one of these and I market for them" — no other agency at his price tier can make this claim. He also has retention/LTV expertise (his own operator edge) that most cleaning agencies ignore because they focus on top of funnel.
5. Alan has 2 paid cleaning clients already ($9,600 revenue). This is his validated revenue niche whether or not it's the "obvious" fit archetype. Money moves faster than opinion.

Top 3 risks if we pick this niche:
- Client archetype tension. Cleaning owners skew female and younger, which is opposite of Alan's stated preferred client archetype (old-school tradesman, 45-60, gruff). This is the discovery-interview flag — if Alan's outreach doesn't click with female cleaning owners, this niche stalls even with a validated offer.
- Per-job ticket is low. Clients who need speed math (roofing/mitigation) can pay $4,800 upfront easily because one closed job pays it back. Cleaning clients need to see months of retention math before they feel the payback. Slower client-payback perception = harder sales conversation.
- Merry Maids / Molly Maid franchise machines dominate the head keywords. Client SEO has to fight for long-tail and neighborhood-level wins.

Top 3 reasons to pick this niche:
- Validated revenue. Alan has already been paid twice. This is the only niche of the three where he has active clients.
- Operator authority is uniquely his. Owns a cleaning company + runs it remotely. Unbeatable positioning against 100% of cleaning-agency competitors at his price tier.
- Retention/LTV expertise. Alan's own edge (from Section 2 of discovery). Adds a $1,000-$2,500/mo retention-service upsell to every cleaning-agency deal.

Money math headline: If we get you 20 new recurring customers a month, and each one is worth $5,200 in their first year plus another $5,200 in their second year, we're adding $208,000 in year-1 booked revenue by month 12. The website and system pays for itself before your third new customer.
